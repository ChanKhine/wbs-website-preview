Our story

We announced an academy for Myanmar’s young people. Within hours, the military seized power.

WBS began as a plan to connect young people abroad with young people inside Myanmar. The coup made it harder and more urgent: a school for democracy, built from abroad.

Why we started

Young people elsewhere are trained to lead. Why not young people from Myanmar?

In Europe and the United States, young people are offered scholarships, training, and places where they are allowed to make mistakes. Governments and institutions invest in the young people who will go home and lead. Our founder, Aye Kari Soe, grew up in the Netherlands and saw this from a young age. She kept asking why Myanmar had nothing like it.

“They invest money and everything in the young people who will go home and lead. I saw that from a young age, and it often saddened me: why don’t I see this in Myanmar?”

Aye Kari Soe, DVB Youth Voice, 6 June 2025 (5:48, translated from Burmese)

In her view, access to education and to positions of power in Myanmar too often went to people connected to the military, while capable young people could not afford a laptop. If a democracy was ever going to last, someone had to prepare a different generation to lead it.

“The next generation have to become leaders… Someone has to become the next president, someone has to become minister of defence, and I don’t want only the elite to become that.”

Aye Kari Soe
Founder & President · Foundation Max van der Stoel, 11 May 2021
31 January 2021

The night we announced the academy

In early 2021 we planned a youth conference and academy in Naypyidaw, to bring young people from Europe and the United States together with young people in Myanmar. We announced it on Instagram and Facebook on the night of 31 January 2021. Within hours, the military seized power. We had to set the plan aside.

After the coup, we did what we could from abroad. We made sure people in the West understood what was happening, and we pressed European politicians to act. But one question kept returning: when this dictatorship ends, who will build what comes after?

How the work grew

From a planned conference to a school for democracy

February 2021

From conference to advocacy

We paused the plan and focused on getting accurate news to audiences in the West, and on lobbying European politicians for action on Myanmar.

April–May 2021

Speaking out in the Netherlands

Our founder presented WBS’s case in The Hague and joined a Foundation Max van der Stoel panel with Dutch MP Kati Piri, about 100 days after the coup.

“We’re gonna end this with our generation, or we’re gonna let the next generation live in fear, grow up in fear… They can shoot, but… they can’t kill the hope that we have.”

Aye Kari Soe, Foundation Max van der Stoel, 11 May 2021 (12:55)
2022

The Diplomatic Academy

We built a training programme in diplomacy, law and politics for young people from Myanmar, taught by practitioners rather than from textbooks.

19 December 2022

Golden Girl Award

YWCA Netherlands honoured our founder for her work on women’s rights and her advocacy for Myanmar.

2023

The Diplomatic Class of 2023

Our 2023 cohort learned from practitioners in diplomacy, human rights, governance and advocacy. See the Academy

2025

The Academy opens to everyone

Free courses, with democracy education at the base, open to everyone. A new group of young people has begun teaching Burmese to the diaspora.

Why education

Our answer starts where the dictatorship does

A dictatorship does not only rule with weapons. It shapes who gets an education and what they are taught. A democracy that lasts has to be learned, so that is where we start.

“What is democracy? What does it mean to show each other love and goodwill? … Right now, everyone needs more education on that. That’s why we’re opening the academy to everyone.”

Aye Kari Soe
DVB Youth Voice, 6 June 2025 (translated from Burmese)
