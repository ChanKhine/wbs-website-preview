Freedom from fear
Why we exist

In February 2021 the military took power by force. We are the generation that refuses to accept it.

WBS was founded on 31 January 2021. That night we announced a youth conference and academy in Naypyidaw, to connect young people abroad with young people in Myanmar. Within hours, the military seized power, detained the country’s elected leaders and ended a decade of fragile democratic reform. Since then it has ruled by force. The elections it staged in 2025–26, held under military control, were widely criticised as neither free nor credible. We have been building that academy from abroad ever since.

“In January–February 2021 we planned to go to Myanmar and hold a youth conference in Naypyidaw. We planned it and posted it on the 31st … Five hours later the crisis happened, the 2021 coup.”

Aye Kari Soe, VOA Burmese, 6 January 2023 (5:07, translated from Burmese)

A dictatorship rules with weapons, but also with fear and silence. So we answer it with knowledge: young people who understand their rights, how democracy works and how to defend it are the hardest to rule against their will.

We are students and young professionals. Some of us left Myanmar. Some of us never got the chance to know it freely. Together we are building the next generation of Burmese leaders, ready to take part in a democracy and to make it last.

Our stand

What we mean by sustainable democracy

A sustainable democracy is one that survives its first crisis. It rests on laws, institutions and informed citizens, not on any single leader or party. Four commitments guide everything we do.

Democracy, not dictatorship

We oppose military rule and every form of dictatorship. Power belongs to the people, exercised through free and fair elections.

The rule of law

Just laws must bind everyone, including those in power. We respect the law, and we help our community understand why lawfulness and justice matter for Myanmar’s future.

Human rights, through peaceful means

We stand for the human rights of everyone in Myanmar, and we work through education, advocacy, diplomacy and peaceful demonstration.

One country, every community

Myanmar’s future has to include all of its peoples. We build connections across its ethnic communities and work for a federal, inclusive democracy.

“Military dictatorship isn’t just Min Aung Hlaing. … The dictatorship’s [machinery] is still there, and that has to be dismantled. Then we rebuild, and not around one person, but with systems.”

Aye Kari Soe
DVB Youth Voice, 6 June 2025 (translated from Burmese)

How we do it

Education first, then advocacy and action

Educate →

The WBS Academy gives young people the knowledge and skills to lead: courses, workshops and mentorships on governance, human rights and diplomacy, plus English classes.

Advocate

Change happens on two fronts: on the ground, and in the rooms where decisions are made. We engage the European Union, the United Nations, international NGOs and Western governments to keep Myanmar on the global agenda and help steer it toward democracy.

Mobilise

We organise workshops, awareness events, online campaigns and peaceful demonstrations to raise visibility and support for democracy and human rights.

Connect

We bring students together with policymakers, diplomats, academics and activists, and we build unity across Myanmar’s ethnic communities and with the wider world.
