Why we teach democracy

Ask a young person in Europe what democracy is and they will probably answer without thinking. Ask the same question in Myanmar, after decades of military rule, and the answer is harder.

“What is democracy? In the West, in Europe, there have been about 500 years of practice. What democracy is, when you walk down the street… it’s in their heads; they know it from birth. But in my view, in Myanmar surroundings: what is democracy? What does it mean to show each other love and goodwill?”

Aye Kari Soe, DVB Youth Voice, 6 June 2025 (10:34, translated from Burmese)

That is why the WBS Academy is now open to everyone, with democracy education at its base. Democracy is not only a form of government. It is a set of habits: how people disagree, how they treat each other, how they hold power to account. Habits like these are learned.

“Right now, everyone needs more education on that. That’s why we’re opening the academy to everyone: join the academy.”

Aye Kari Soe, DVB Youth Voice, 6 June 2025 (11:07, translated from Burmese)
