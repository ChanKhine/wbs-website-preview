The WBS Academy

Democracy has to be taught. This is where we start.

Short courses, workshops and mentorships that make democracy, human rights and ethical governance simple, practical and something you can act on, open to students, professionals and volunteers wherever they are.

Whether you’re a student, a professional, or a volunteer —

If you want to help build a democratic Myanmar, there is a place for you here. Learn, connect and build stronger communities from wherever you are.

What our students learn

Knowledge that makes democracy last

Topics from our programmes so far. A democracy needs citizens who understand it, so this is where we start.

Democracy and governance

What ethical governance looks like, and what it takes to build institutions that serve people.

Human rights and the rule of law

Why human rights and just laws matter, and why they must apply to everyone.

Diplomacy & advocacy

What diplomacy is, how the United Nations and global institutions work, and how to engage European countries and the US.

Skills to lead

Confident English, critical thinking, setting and applying SMART goals, managing money, and speaking up without fear of mistakes.

How we teach

Taught by people who do the work

Our teachers are practitioners in diplomacy, human rights, journalism and campaigning, not only textbook authors.

Practitioners, not only textbooks

Working diplomats, trainers who train diplomats, journalists and campaigners explain how the work is really done, then set a challenge: how would you apply this to Myanmar?

Rooted in what Myanmar needs

Speakers who know the situation inside Myanmar explain what is needed now, so that what we learn can turn into projects that help.

Free, open and built to last

Free courses, open to everyone, with democracy education at the base. This is a long-term project, not a one-off course.

Past programme

WBS English Summer Course

Our most recent programme, the WBS English Summer Course, ran twice a week with over 60 students registered: practical English learning built around real-world discussion topics, taught by our own community. Why English? Much of the work of advocacy and diplomacy happens in it, so speaking with confidence is a political skill as well as a language skill.

Knowledge Gained

  • What diplomacy is, and the role of the United Nations and global institutions
  • European countries and the US
  • Managing money
  • Setting and applying SMART goals

Skills Developed

  • Introducing themselves in English
  • Expressing themselves with confidence
  • Speaking up and not being afraid to make mistakes
  • Thinking critically
  • Talking about mental health
